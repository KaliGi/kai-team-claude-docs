    Submissions¶

    A submission round produces two distinct kinds of artefact, and they belong in two distinct places. The journal-facing record (what you sent, what you received) lives in project_management/submissions/<NN_journal_round>/, gitignored and version-frozen. The collaborative working area (the response-to-referees TeX you and your co-authors edit together) lives in paper/revisions/<JOURNAL>/, git-tracked and Overleaf-synced. Mixing the two breaks the workflow: the journal record stops being immutable, or the response stops reaching your co-authors through Overleaf.

    The two homes — and the split¶

    Artefact Where Why
    Submitted paper PDF (version-frozen) project_management/submissions/<NN_journal_round>/ Immutable archive of what you sent
    Cover letter PDF (version-frozen) project_management/submissions/<NN_journal_round>/ Immutable archive
    Response-to-referees PDF (R&R+) project_management/submissions/<NN_journal_round>/ Immutable archive
    Referee reports as received (journal PDFs / txts) project_management/submissions/<NN_journal_round>/ Original journal correspondence
    Editor letter as received project_management/submissions/<NN_journal_round>/ Original journal correspondence
    Cover letter TeX source (editable, per round) project_management/submissions/<NN_journal_round>/overleaf/ Submission-specific; usually drafted by the author of record
    Response-to-referees TeX source paper/revisions/<JOURNAL>/ Collaborative — co-authors edit it together in Overleaf
    Agent-formatted referee reports (one TeX per referee) paper/revisions/<JOURNAL>/ Lives next to the response so the response can \input it
    Editor letter formatted TeX (optional) paper/revisions/<JOURNAL>/ Same workflow as the referee reports
    Submission log (one line per round) project_management/submission_log.md Navigation index across rounds

    A reader who already knows the rule can stop here and skip to the worked example. The sections below explain why the split exists, what goes inside each home, and what does NOT go in either.

    Why the split¶

    The submission round's working files split because paper/ is the co-author-shared surface and project_management/ is the author-of-record-private surface. paper/ is what gets pushed to GitHub and synced into Overleaf, so anything that lives there reaches every co-author the moment you push — and they can mark it up in Overleaf's track-changes view, the standard collaboration workflow for a response. project_management/ is gitignored: it lives on your laptop and never reaches anyone else.

    The response-to-referees is the central collaborative artefact of a revision — each co-author owns parts of it, marks up parts of others' drafts, and the team negotiates the final framing in track-changes. That work belongs in paper/revisions/<JOURNAL>/ so Overleaf can pick it up. The cover letter is shorter, editor-facing, and usually drafted by the author of record alone; it belongs with the submission packet for that round.

    The originals (the submitted PDF, the referee PDFs as the journal sent them, the editor letter) stay in project_management/submissions/ because they are frozen documentary record. You may read the referee PDF a hundred times during a revision; you should never edit it. I never touch a submitted PDF after it goes in, even to fix an error I spot later: it is the record of exactly what the referees and editor saw, and the corrected version belongs in the next round's folder.

    Where the response-to-referees TeX lives — and why per-journal¶

    paper/revisions/<JOURNAL>/ mirrors the paper/presentations/<venue>_<date>/ convention from the folder structure: per-venue subfolder by short name, not by round number. If the paper goes through three rounds at QJE, all three rounds' working response files live in paper/revisions/QJE/ and git history carries the per-round versions. If the paper is then rejected at QJE and resubmitted to RESTAT, a fresh paper/revisions/RESTAT/ workspace is created — the QJE folder stays as the historical record of what happened there.

    The per-journal grouping matches how co-authors think about the revision: "we're working on the QJE response," not "we're working on round 2." It also makes the response TeX easy to find in a year-old project where you've forgotten the round numbering.

    Worked example — a paper at QJE R&R after one initial submission¶

    The shape after one R&R round:

    project_management/submissions/
├── 01_QJE_initial/              (initial submission to QJE)
│   ├── paper_v_submitted.pdf
│   ├── cover_letter.pdf
│   └── overleaf/
│       └── cover_letter.tex
└── 02_QJE_R&R/                  (R&R round at QJE)
    ├── paper_v_resubmitted.pdf
    ├── cover_letter.pdf
    ├── response_to_referees.pdf   (the frozen PDF)
    ├── referee_1.pdf              (as received from journal)
    ├── referee_2.pdf              (as received from journal)
    ├── editor_letter.pdf          (as received)
    └── overleaf/
        └── cover_letter.tex       (R&R cover letter — short, editor-facing)

paper/
└── revisions/
    └── QJE/                       (Overleaf-synced revision workspace)
        ├── response_to_referees.tex
        ├── referee_1_formatted.tex   (agent-generated from referee_1.pdf)
        ├── referee_2_formatted.tex   (agent-generated from referee_2.pdf)
        └── editor_letter_formatted.tex

    Notice the split: the response TeX is in paper/revisions/QJE/, the response PDF is frozen in project_management/submissions/02_QJE_R&R/. The referee PDFs (as received) are in submissions; the formatted referee TeX (with structured comment numbers for the response to address) is in paper/revisions/QJE/. The cover letter PDF + its Overleaf TeX source stay together in submissions because the cover letter is per-round and author-of-record-private.

    If the QJE rejects and you resubmit to RESTAT, the next state adds:

    project_management/submissions/
└── 03_RESTAT_initial/
    └── ...

paper/revisions/
├── QJE/                          (historical — stays as the QJE record)
└── RESTAT/                       (fresh workspace)
    └── ...

    The QJE workspace is not deleted; it documents what the QJE round looked like in case you need to retrace the framing decisions when responding to RESTAT.

    Naming convention¶

    Per-round subfolder is <NN>_<journal>_<round>. The <NN> keeps rounds sortable in disk order; <journal> keeps them recognisable; <round> distinguishes initial from revision rounds (and helps when the same journal sees two rounds).

    Examples:

    • 01_QJE_initial — first submission, QJE
    • 02_QJE_R&R — R&R round at QJE
    • 03_QJE_R&R2 — second R&R at QJE (rare but happens)
    • 04_AER_initial — paper rejected at QJE, resubmitted to AER

    The & in R&R is a real character in the folder name; on Windows that is fine, on filesystems that complain you can use R-and-R. Match what your filesystem accepts and apply it consistently across rounds.

    Why gitignored¶

    project_management/submissions/ is gitignored for three reasons:

    • Strategic framing. Cover letters and response-to-referees often contain reviewer-anticipation reasoning, competitor-paper comparisons, and framing decisions that the author of record drafts before sharing with co-authors. Until that draft is ready to share, it should not auto-propagate via git.
    • Confidential journal correspondence. Some editor letters explicitly request confidentiality. The referee PDFs are usually marked confidential by the journal regardless. Keeping them out of git means they do not accidentally end up on a public GitHub fork.
    • The journal portal is the official record. This folder is the working copy of the submission packet, not the canonical one. The journal's portal is where the official version lives; this folder is for you to read and re-read during the revision.

    The collaborative half — paper/revisions/<JOURNAL>/ — is tracked in git, because the whole point of putting the response there is to share it with co-authors via Overleaf. The split is what lets both rules hold at once.

    The submission log¶

    project_management/submission_log.md is the one-line-per-round navigation index across the whole project's submission history. One line per round: date, journal, round type, outcome. Live, hand-maintained, short.

    Example:

    # Submission log

| Date submitted | Journal | Round | Outcome | Folder |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2026-01-10 | QJE | initial | R&R (2026-04-22) | 01_QJE_initial/ |
| 2026-06-15 | QJE | R&R     | ... pending     | 02_QJE_R&R/      |

    The log answers "what's the current status of this paper?" at a glance. The detailed materials live in the per-round subfolders; the log is the table of contents.

    What does NOT live in either home¶

    A few things look like submission materials but are not:

    • Internal pre-submission feedback (mock referee from a colleague, talks-feedback that fed into the framing) — those are research-process notes and go in project_management/notes_and_feedback/ with date prefixes per the notes-and-feedback page. The boundary: anything you wrote or received as part of your own collaboration process, not the journal's response.
    • Drafts of the manuscript between rounds. The manuscript is in paper/paper.tex with git history carrying the rounds. There is no separate "draft of round 2" file; round 2 is the state of paper/paper.tex when you submitted it. The frozen PDF in submissions/02_QJE_R&R/ is the snapshot.
    • Grant proposals that fund the work. Grants live in their own folder outside the project per the grants page.
    • Talks built from the paper (seminar slides, conference decks). Those go in paper/presentations/<venue>_<date>/, not in a submissions or revisions folder.

    Disclosure statements the journal will require (added 2026-07)¶

    Two compliance items are now standard at submission time and belong in every pre-submission checklist:

    • AI-use disclosure. Elsevier journals (JDE, JPubE, EJPE, Journal of Econometrics, and the rest of the family) require authors who used generative AI in the writing process to insert a "Declaration of generative AI and AI-assisted technologies in the writing process" section immediately above the references, using the publisher's template sentence; AI may be used "to improve the language and readability" and cannot be listed as an author (Elsevier generative-AI policy). Springer Nature, Wiley, Taylor and Francis, and SAGE have similar disclosure requirements. The AEA journal-policies page carries no generative-AI policy as of 2026-07 (AEA policies), so AEA submissions currently need no AI statement -- re-check at submission time.
    • Conflict-of-interest / funding statements. The AEA Disclosure Policy is mandatory for every author: each author submits a disclosure statement identifying potential conflicts of interest, and authors with nothing to disclose must say so explicitly (AEA Disclosure Policy). Most other publishers require an equivalent declaration plus a funding acknowledgement.

    Practical rule: when assembling a submission packet, look up the target journal's publisher, apply its AI-disclosure template if one exists, and verify a COI/funding statement exists for every author before the packet freezes. Both are cheap to include and expensive to be caught missing.

    Cross-cutting with the toolkit¶

    The submission workflow integrates with several existing skills:

    • /review-paper — 6-agent pre-submission referee panel before sending. Run it on the manuscript before the submitted PDF is frozen.
    • /review-paper-light — the fast pre-screen variant when you want a quick sanity check rather than the full panel.
    • bib-audit — catches hallucinated, chimeric, or otherwise broken references in references.bib before the journal does. Run it as a pre-submission gate.
    • audit-reproducibility — confirms the paper's numeric claims match the actual code outputs before the submitted version freezes. Run it on every round; the cost of a mismatch grows after a referee notices it.
    • /respond-to-referees (queued, not yet shipped) — agent-formatting of referee PDFs into the structured TeX that goes in paper/revisions/<JOURNAL>/. Until it ships, the formatting is manual.

    Anti-patterns¶

    • Submitted PDFs in paper/old/. Loses the per-round bundling with the cover letter and the response. The submitted PDF, cover letter, response, and journal correspondence all belong together as one round's bundle.
    • Cover letters in random root-level files. Every submission's cover letter is per-round and lives in that round's submissions/<NN_journal_round>/ folder, with its editable TeX in the overleaf/ subfolder.
    • Editing a submitted PDF after submission. Version freeze means freeze. The PDF you sent is what the referees and editor saw; if it contained an error, it is part of the historical record. The next round's PDF (in the next submissions/<NN>/ folder) is where the corrected version lives.
    • Response-to-referees TeX in project_management/. Defeats the Overleaf-collaborative workflow; co-authors never see your draft until you email them the file. Put it in paper/revisions/<JOURNAL>/ so the Overleaf sync makes it available the moment you push.
    • Referee reports filed under notes_and_feedback/. Referee reports are journal correspondence, not internal discussion notes. They go in project_management/submissions/<NN_journal_round>/ (the as-received PDFs) and, in formatted form, in paper/revisions/<JOURNAL>/.
    • Numbering rounds without <journal> in the name. 01/, 02/, 03/ makes a one-paper project briefly tidy and a three-paper, two-rejections-and-an-R&R history impossible to navigate. Keep <NN>_<journal>_<round> everywhere.
