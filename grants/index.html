    Grants¶

    Scope. This page is deliberately light. The firm rule is where grant proposals live; the internal layout is a starting point, not prescriptive. The recommendation is what has worked in practice, not a claim to be the best possible approach.

    The rule¶

    A grant proposal lives in its own folder, outside any research project. Treat it as a small writing project: its own bibliography, its own panel-interview slides, its own submission round (the funder is the journal-equivalent). I keep every grant in a dedicated folder of its own rather than tucked inside the project it might fund, and that one habit has saved me the most grief when a project moves on but the grant's paperwork is still live.

    Why a separate folder¶

    A grant has its own lifecycle, and that lifecycle does not match any one paper. It draws on past research, seeds future research, and outlives any single project. Mixing a grant into a paper folder breaks the paper's clean structure; mixing two grants into a single folder loses the per-grant submission history.

    The other half of the case is administrative. Grants come with paperwork (funder portals, signed forms, no-cost extensions, audit requirements) that keeps showing up for years after the grant runs out. Keeping it in a self-contained folder means a final-report search five years on still finds everything in one place.

    Recommended internal layout (starting point)¶

    The kit's paper layout applies, scaled down:

    grant_<funder>_<NN>/
├── proposal.tex                  main writeup
├── references.bib
├── output/                       figures / tables for the proposal
├── presentations/                slides for the panel interview, if any
├── project_management/
│   ├── notes_and_feedback/       feedback from mock review, internal critique
│   ├── IRB/                      if the grant requires ethics paperwork up front
│   └── admin/                    application paperwork, signed forms, funder correspondence
└── literature/                   reading specific to this proposal

    Skip whatever the grant does not generate. A small proposal with no panel-interview talk does not create presentations/; a proposal that does not touch human subjects does not create IRB/.

    Naming the folder¶

    grant_<funder>_<NN> where <funder> is the funder short name (SNF, ERC, NSF, DFG) and <NN> is your two-digit grant counter — e.g. grant_SNF_01, grant_ERC_02. The counter lets you keep history when the same funder is applied to twice. Add _<topic> if a single counter slot is ambiguous (rare): grant_SNF_03_diaspora.

    Where these folders live as a group is up to you. Many people keep all grants in one parent folder (grant_applications/ or similar) so they sit together for portfolio review.

    When the grant funds, paperwork stays with the grant¶

    When a grant is funded, the funded paperwork (award letter, budget revisions, milestone reports, no-cost extension requests, final report) stays in this grant folder under project_management/admin/. Some of that paperwork also belongs in a downstream paper project's project_management/admin/ (e.g. a co-author NDA tied to that paper). That is a copy-not-move case — the grant folder stays the canonical home for grant-specific paperwork.

    Anti-patterns¶

    • Putting a grant folder inside a research project's project_management/. The grant outlives the project; nesting it loses the grant when the project closes.
    • One folder for multiple grants. Loses the per-grant submission history and forces ambiguous filenames.
    • Treating the grant as an unstructured proposal_v3.docx in a flat folder. A grant has its own references, figures, and review feedback — at minimum a proposal file, a references file, and somewhere for feedback.
    • Leaving the funded paperwork in your email when the grant funds. Pull it into admin/ once the award letter lands so the project has the artefact, not just you.

    Worked example reference¶

    Kai's SNSF application followed an earlier version of this layout, before the kit's current conventions hardened. Treat it as a real-world reference, not as the ideal — newer grants should follow the layout above.
